Nike Kobe 3 Low Protro Performance Review

We at WearTesters usually prefer the low top version of a shoe over its high top counterpart, but the Nike Kobe 3 Low Protro may be the exception.

Nike Kobe 3 Low Protro

Release Date: 03/12/2026

Price: $180

Sizing: True to size

Buy Nike Kobe 3 Low Protro
  • Summary: The Nike Kobe 3 Low Protro is nearly identical to its high top counterpart, but a lack of containment makes these an inferior on-court option.

Pros

  • Top tier cushion
  • Breathability and flexibility
  • Looks awesome

Cons

  • Lack of lateral containment and support
  • Inconsistent traction with translucent outsole

Traction

The Nike Kobe 3 Low Protro uses the same exact traction pattern as the high, which is a slightly modified version of the original Kobe 3 traction pattern. Traction performance on the Kobe 3 Protro was great, so it should be the same here, right?

Wrong – the traction on these is inconsistent. There was more slipping and worse performance on dusty courts than the highs, and this is likely due to the rubber compound. This pair has a translucent rubber outsole, and Nike’s translucent rubber isn’t consistent performance-wise. If you’re able to get a pair with a solid rubber outsole, we’d recommend buying those, since the performance of the solid rubber outsole on the highs was excellent.

If you’re playing outdoors, this outsole will be durable enough for extended use, and the translucent outsole should work just fine in terms of performance.

Cushion

The Nike Kobe 3 Low Protro has the exact same cushion setup as the highs, and unlike the traction performance, these feel just as good as the highs do. The midsole is comprised of Cushlon 3.0 foam, and there’s a full length Zoom Strobel unit in there as well. It’s bouncy, smooth, and also housed within a rubber cupsole, which helps keep it stable. Overall, it’s one of the best cushion setups you can get.

One thing to note is that the footbed sits higher off the floor than other Nike basketball models, so if court feel is more of a priority than cushion for you, you may want to consider other options.

Materials

Not to sound like a broken record, but the materials on the Nike Kobe 3 Low Protro are, you guessed it, the same as what’s used on the highs. The upper uses a mesh build with rubberized netting layered over the top – this gives the upper some structure while maintaining airflow and flexibility. You also have a leather toe cap, with additional leather on the tongue and the back of the shoe.

The difference in materials from the lows to the highs comes solely from the collar height, which actually makes a difference in performance and support this time around. More on that in a second.

Fit

The Nike Kobe 3 Low Protro fits true to size, but there are issues with containment. Despite having the same upper construction as the highs, the difference in collar height makes a difference in terms of lockdown. Having extra lace loops and material to work with on the highs made the fit more customizable and secure, which led to better lockdown throughout the shoe (though it did become a bit restrictive around the ankle, which can be the downside of wearing a high top).

With these, I was never able to get sufficient lockdown in the heel or from a lateral perspective, so my foot felt like it was going to slide over the footbed on every movement. That’s a dealbreaker when it comes to fit.

Support

The Nike Kobe 3 Low Protro has plenty of support features, like a carbon fiber midfoot shank plate and a strong heel counter. Those things don’t matter, though, when the shoe has a sloppy fit. Those features are also present on the high tops, so if you want support, go with those.

Nike Kobe 3 Low Protro Summary

While the Nike Kobe 3 Low Protro is pretty much identical to the high top version of the shoe, with the exception of collar height, that difference in collar height makes a huge difference in lockdown. Outside of the fit, these have a lot of great features, particularly the cushion. There are a ton of great cushion setups from various brands out there, but when done right, it’s hard to beat full-length Zoom Air.

Overall, if you’re looking for a performance basketball shoe, opt for the high tops over these.
